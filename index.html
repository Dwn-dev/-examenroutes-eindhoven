Examiner favourites

the spots people fail on
Hovenring (Noord-Brabantlaan × Heerbaan × Meerhovendreef)The famous floating bicycle roundabout. Cars circulate below while the bike ring hovers above — lane choice on approach is everything, and it sits minutes from the CBR, so expect it early in your exam. Routes 2, 3 & 4 cross it.
Signalised roundaboutsEindhoven's speciality: multi-lane roundabouts with traffic lights and cyclists who usually have priority. A green light does not cancel the zebra/bike crossing on exit. Scan the crossing every single exit.
Flight Forum roundabouts (airport)Big, busy, two-lane, full of taxis and hurried drivers. Pick your lane before entering, hold it, signal your exit.
N2 parallel lanes (weven)The Randweg's local N2 lanes mean short merges, weaving traffic and exits that come fast. Mirror–signal–shoulder check, commit, keep your speed up.
Bus lanes on Noord-BrabantlaanHOV lanes run along it. Never use them, and watch for buses getting priority at lights.
30 km/h zones & uitritconstructiesZeelst, Hanevoet, Bennekel, Grasrijk: brick streets, exit constructions (you have priority leaving them — but pedestrians crossing the sidewalk you cross don't yield to you), and right-of-way from the right. Crawl the speed, exaggerate the head movement.

Car questions — binnen & buiten

tap a question to reveal
How this part works

Right after the eye test, the examiner asks one or two questions about the car — drawn from three flavours: outside the car, under the bonnet, and inside the car. For the lights there's a set procedure: you sit behind the wheel, turn the ignition on without starting, and operate the dip beam, rear lights, brake lights and indicators while they check outside. Crucial: every answer must match your lesson car — rehearse these on that exact car until you can point blind.

Buiten de auto & onder de motorkap

  • Tread at least 1,6 mm across the main grooves — show the wear indicators (small bumps inside the grooves) or a depth gauge. Also glance for damage, bulges and the valve cap.
  • The sticker in the door frame or fuel flap (or the manual) — and pressure is checked on cold tyres.
  • Reverse towards a window, garage door or another car, press the brake, and check the reflection. Same trick for the reversing light.
  • Dip beams, tail lights, brake lights (incl. the third one), indicators, reversing light, number-plate light and the reflectors — all whole, working and fixed. Same rule for the plates: intact, readable, secure.
  • Leaks or obstacles (children, animals) under it, whether it sits level (a lean can mean a flat), clean windows and mirrors, bonnet and boot properly shut.
  • Cold engine, car on level ground: pull the dipstick, wipe it, dip again, read — the level must sit between min and max. That min–max rule goes for all the fluids.
  • Know the three reservoirs in your lesson car by sight. Coolant and brake fluid read min–max on the outside of the tank — and never open the coolant cap on a hot engine.

Binnen de auto

  • The rear-screen heating button — rectangle with wavy arrows. It switches itself off after a few minutes.
  • Airflow to the windscreen, blower up, A/C on (it dries the air), rear heater on too. Do it without hunting for buttons.
  • Only when visibility is under 50 m — and never for rain alone. Front fog lights: fog, snowfall or rain that seriously limits visibility.
  • Off, auto (sensor picks for you), stadslicht/position, dimlicht — with groot licht on the stalk. Know every position blind.
  • Red means stop safely as soon as possible and investigate — the oil can (oil pressure) and thermometer (coolant) mean engine off, now.
  • A warning, not an emergency — like the engine-management light: you can usually drive on calmly, but book the garage soon.
  • Flip the anti-glare tab under the interior mirror. Flip it back in daylight.
  • Usually a key switch in the glovebox or door edge — off when a rear-facing child seat sits in front. A lit airbag light otherwise means a system fault.
  • Point instantly, eyes closed. If you have to search for them in calm conditions, you won't find them in a moment of stress.

Bijzondere verrichtingen

3 assignments · 8 techniques
How the system works

The exam has three assignments — omkeeropdracht (turn the car around), parkeeropdracht (park as close as possible to a named spot) and stopopdracht (stop tightly behind another vehicle). You get two of the three, and at least one must involve reversing. The hellingproef is an occasional extra. The examiner names the goal; you choose the place and the method — and that choice is itself scored. Passed these at a tussentijdse toets? Then you're exempt at the exam. The 8 techniques below are your toolbox.

1 · Recht achteruitrijden — straight reverse

Walking pace, primary view through the rear window, kerb about 30 cm away, constant scanning all around.

Waar hier: quiet straight stretches — Croy & De Hurk side streets, Grasrijk's new grid.

2 · Achteruit een bocht — reverse around a corner

Keep the kerb distance constant through the arc, steer in millimetres, and give way to absolutely everything — you're the intruder here.

Waar hier: wide, quiet corners — business-park junctions and the Hanevoet loops.

3 · Steken — three-point turn

Full lock left to the far kerb, reverse on full right lock, drive off. Look before every direction change; traffic in the street has priority over you throughout.

Waar hier: mid-width residential streets — Bennekel, Ekenrooi, the Zeelst core.

4 · Halve draai — one-arc U-turn

Only where the street is wide enough to make it in one; decide before you start, then commit — stalling broadside is the fail mode.

Waar hier: broad quiet streets — business parks and Veldhoven's wider woonstraten.

5 · Vakparkeren — bay park (forward or reverse)

Your choice unless specified; reversing in gives the safer exit later. Mirror on the bay line, slow wheels + fast steering, scan mirror–shoulder–mirror. A calm visible correction beats a lucky swing.

Waar hier: Grasrijk's marked bays, Zeelst around the shops and church, Citycentrum edges — and very often the CBR car park itself at the finish.

6 · Fileparkeren — parallel park

Level with the front car, about a metre out; reverse until your rear wheel is at their bumper, full lock in, 45°, lock out, creep straight. A second go is fine — hindering traffic or not looking is what costs.

Waar hier: streets with parked rows — Bennekel, Zeelst, Aalst/Ekenrooi.

7 · Stopopdracht — precision stop

Stop as short as possible behind a parked vehicle (left or right side both allowed), secure the car, then pull away with the full look-around ritual.

Waar hier: any calm street with kerbside cars — usually in the same quiet pocket as your other assignment.

8 · Hellingproef — hill start

Handbrake on, find the bite, ease off as you feel the nose lift, no roll-back. Asked only occasionally.

Waar hier: flat Eindhoven barely has one — at most a viaduct or parking-ramp. Drill the routine once so it's automatic anyway.

Is there a location pattern?

No published one — the system is deliberately location-free, because you pick the spot and that pick is judged. But three real patterns exist. First: manoeuvres come mid-ride when the examiner steers into a quiet pocket, and on these routes those pockets are Croy/De Hurk, Zeelst, Grasrijk, Hanevoet/Bennekel, Ekenrooi and Veldhoven's woonstraten — so practise the techniques inside those exact areas. Second: parking assignments gravitate to real destinations ("park as close as possible to the entrance of…"), and rides very often end with a park on the CBR terrain — treat the final approach to Hoevenweg as still-scored. Third: since spot choice is the tested skill, drill choosing: an adequate manoeuvre in a well-chosen place beats a perfect one somewhere awkward. The Croy streets are nearly empty after 18:00 and at weekends — rehearse where you'll perform.

Pocket rules

the ones that decide exams

Speeds around here

  • 30 — village cores & residential grids: Zeelst, Oerle, Grasrijk, Hanevoet
  • 50 — city default (bebouwde kom)
  • 70 — only where signed on the big avenues
  • 60 / 80 — country lanes / N-roads outside town
  • 100 — motorway by day (06:00–19:00); matrix signs always override

Priority — the classic fails

  • Equal junction: right goes first — constant in Zeelst & Oerle
  • Leaving an uitrit: you yield to everyone, pavement included
  • Roundabouts: yield to traffic on it, signal right on every exit
  • Zebra: also stop for pedestrians clearly about to cross
  • Bus pulling out in town: let it go
  • Yellow signs & markings beat the permanent white ones

What the examiner will say

Dutch commands, decoded
  • "De volgende straat rechts / links"Next street right/left — mirror, signal, position early. Not sure which street counts? Ask. Asking is free.
  • "Bij de rotonde de derde afslag"Third exit at the roundabout — count every exit as you pass it, and signal right before yours.
  • "Gewoon de weg volgen"Just follow the road — no turns coming, but priority rules still apply at every junction.
  • "Volg vanaf hier de navigatie"The independent-driving block starts. You're judged on safe driving, not perfect route-following — a wrong turn corrected safely costs nothing.
  • "Keer het voertuig"Turn the car around — the method is usually yours to choose. Pick your best one and commit.
  • "Zoek zelf een parkeerplaats"Find your own spot and park — choosing an easy one is allowed, and smart.
  • "Stop hier maar even"The precision stop — mirrors, signal, tight to the kerb, secure the car.
  • "Rijd maar weer weg"Move off — the full ritual: mirrors, blind spot, signal, go.
  • "Dat bespreken we na afloop""We'll discuss it afterwards" — standard deflection, not a verdict. Don't read doom into it; keep driving.
